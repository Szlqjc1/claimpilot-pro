# Scope: what else is in the photo

**Question.** The upload path crops to the page it detects (D-50) before OCR. What happens when the photo holds more
than one invoice, or other printed text, or not quite all of the invoice?

**Answer.** Background text is not a problem: the crop removes it. A second invoice is: the crop picks the larger
page, so with the other invoice nearer the camera the app reads **the wrong document, with full confidence**. In this
benchmark that never reached an auto-approval, but only because the claim's own record disagrees with the other
invoice (R-20), and the vendor master with its bank details (R-17). A missing edge of a few percent cost nothing.

| Prediction (before the run) | Result |
|---|---|
| S2 mostly passes with rectification on: the crop removes the background | **Holds, fully.** 20/20 cropped to the invoice; 0 of the other document's 100 values in the OCR text, against 54/100 (in 20/20 documents) with rectification off. |
| S1 can fail silently: both pages are plausible, and "the largest page" may be the wrong one | **The crop fails as predicted** (wrong page 11/20: all 10 where it was nearer, plus 1 of 10 where it was 4 % smaller). **Silent, no:** live, the model bound all five wrong values on those 11, and all 11 went to review (R-20 on 11, R-17 on 11, R-02 on 10). 0 auto-approved with a wrong binding. |

## Method

- **Documents:** the same 20 seeded claims as every other benchmark (auto-clear on their reference extraction).
- **The other document** (S1, S2): the next seeded claim by ID whose five critical values (vendor, invoice number,
  date, PO, total) all differ from the target's and that has a PO, so every field has a wrong value to bind. In S1
  the target is the **left** page; that is the ground truth.
- **Scenes** (`src/lib/bench/scope.ts`, `scope1`): canonical renders at ≈ 300 dpi, each sheet warped into a quad on
  the D-47 desk, uneven light 0.3.
  - **S0:** one page at 82 % of the frame, pitch 8° / yaw 6° / roll 2° (a careful phone photo).
  - **S1:** landscape frame, two pages side by side, each ≈ 44 % of the frame width with desk between them. The
    target page is 4 % larger on even-numbered documents, the other page on odd ones.
  - **S2:** the invoice at 60 % of the frame; a shipping label on the desk beside it (the other document's vendor,
    PO, invoice number as "Ref", date, declared value); another printed invoice's top-left corner reaching into the
    frame, rotated 15°.
  - **S3:** the page at 90 % of the frame, shifted right so 3 % of its width is outside the frame.
- **OCR:** the upload path exactly: page detection and rectification (D-50, `corners-v3`), illumination
  normalisation (D-52), grayscale clean-up, Tesseract `eng` PSM 3, word boxes mapped back onto the photo.
- **Metrics, model-free:**
  - which region the detector chose (mean corner distance within 3 % of the frame's shorter side of the target
    sheet, the other sheet, or neither; for S3 the visible part of the target);
  - CER against the target's transcript;
  - the target's critical values found by the grounding matchers;
  - R-14 / R-19 with the target's known-good extraction substituted for the model's;
  - the other document's critical values present in the OCR text: what a model could bind.
- **Wrong-document bindings, live** (S1 only, 20 calls): extracted values equal to the other document's and not
  the target's, scored by the real engine with the D-55 word-box presence.

## What this means

- **One crop, by area.** Two complete pages in one photo are read as one, and which one is chosen depends on area:
  in practice, which page was nearer the camera. Nothing in the OCR output marks the choice. Page confidence on the
  wrong page averaged 0.918, against 0.916 on the right one, and the `ocr.rectify` log says `page · rectified` either way.
- **What caught it** is the claim, not the image. The other invoice's vendor, invoice number and total disagree with
  what the claim records (R-20), and its remit-to bank with the claimed vendor's master (R-17). A claim whose own
  record was typed from the wrong invoice would not be caught this way; that case is out of scope for an image check.
- **Background text never reached the OCR text with the crop on.** It does with the crop off (54 of the other
  document's 100 values, in every document), so `OCR_RECTIFY=off` reintroduces it.
- **S3:** with 3 % of the page outside the frame the detector still finds a quad on the visible part (the frame edge
  is its right side), and all 98 values were read. The template has a right margin wider than 3 %. A deeper cut that
  removes text would cost those values, and R-14 / R-19 then apply as for any unreadable field.
- **S0** reproduces the clean-scan control exactly (CER 3.6 %, 98/98, confidence 0.920; ILLUMINATION.md).

## Limits

- Synthetic scenes on our renders, as in PERSPECTIVE.md: straight page edges, no curl, no blur, no sensor noise.
- S1 used two pages of equal size and a 4 % difference; real photos differ more, which makes the choice more
  predictable, not more correct.
- The S1 live check ran with rectification on. With rectification off both documents' values are in the text (90 of
  100); which ones the model then binds was not measured.
- 20 documents per arm: a 0/20 has a 95 % upper bound of about 14 %.

## Reproduce

```bash
pnpm bench:scope
```

Needs `./eng.traineddata` (exits 0 with a message otherwise). `--live S1` adds the wrong-binding check (at most 20
model calls per arm; needs `AI_MODE=live` and a key). Scenes and OCR are cached under `.bench-cache/`, so a re-run
makes no OCR or model calls. Raw rows: [`docs/scope.csv`](scope.csv). The live S1 run made 20 calls, 22,764 input and
10,414 output tokens.
