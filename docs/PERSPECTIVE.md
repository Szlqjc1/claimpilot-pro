# Perspective: is image rectification worth building?

**Question.** A phone photo of an invoice is a tilted page on a desk. If extraction degrades, is the loss caused by
the geometry, which corner detection plus rectification would fix, or by everything else a photo does to the
pixels (uneven light, resampling, lower effective resolution), which rectification cannot fix?

## Decision rule (written before the run)

This section was written and saved before any perspective photo was OCR'd or sent to the model.

**Outcome per document.** Each of the 20 documents is a seeded claim that auto-clears correctly from its reference
extraction (the same 20 as `docs/EXTRACTION_VARIANTS.md`). On the canonical render, each one auto-clears with every
critical field correct. Any other outcome is **bad**:
- escalated (any finding, including R-14 / R-19 / R-20), or
- auto-cleared with a wrong or missing critical field, or
- the extraction failed.

```
bad(arm)   = documents with a bad outcome / 20          ("error_or_escalation")
gap        = bad(A1) − bad(A0)
recovered  = bad(A1) − bad(A2)
recovery%  = recovered / gap
```

The decision uses the **harsher pair**: A1 at pitch 20° / yaw 15° and its oracle A2. The 10° / 10° pair is reported
alongside, but does not decide.

| Result | Decision |
|---|---|
| recovery% ≥ 70 % | **Build corner detection** (1–2 days): geometry is the bottleneck. |
| recovery% ≤ 30 % (or negative) | **Do not build it**: the OCR, not the geometry, is the bottleneck. |
| between | Report it; recommend a **spike**, not a build. |
| gap ≤ 1 document (≤ 5 %) | Perspective costs nothing measurable at these angles; recovery% is undefined. **Do not build.** |

The three columns of the variant benchmark are still reported per arm (field accuracy, escalation rate, error in
auto-cleared claims). `bad` only combines them for this one decision.

## Arms

| Arm | Input to OCR |
|---|---|
| **A0** baseline | the canonical render (canonical labels, label-left layout), as in `EXTRACTION_VARIANTS.md` |
| **A1** pitch 10 / yaw 10 | the render, photographed |
| **A1** pitch 20 / yaw 15 | the render, photographed |
| **A2** oracle (10/10 and 20/15) | the A1 photo, warped back with `mat3Inverse(groundTruth)` before OCR |

Photo parameters (`src/lib/bench/photo.ts`, deterministic):
- pinhole camera about 0.8 frame diagonals from the page (≈ 64° diagonal field of view, like a phone);
- the unrotated page spans 80 % of the frame;
- roll 2°;
- page-centre offset seeded per document, within ±2 % of the frame;
- seeded wood-grain desk background;
- uneven light at strength 0.3 (the darkest corner at about 70 % brightness), seeded per document.

The photo has the render's pixel size (≈ 2550 × 3300). OCR is the app's own pipeline (Tesseract `eng`, grayscale
clean-up with deskew, PSM 3), and extraction the live model with prompt `extract-v2`, as in every other arm.

A2 is deliberately "cheating": it uses the true corners, which a real system would have to detect. It measures the
**ceiling** of image correction, separately from how hard it is to find the corners. A2 keeps the uneven light and
the two resamplings (photo, then rectification). The only thing it removes is the geometry.

## What broke first, and where the bottleneck is

**The geometry, and almost entirely the geometry.** Already at the milder tilt (pitch 10° / yaw 10°), 11 of 20
documents stopped being a correct auto-clear. Field accuracy fell from 100 % to 92 % (84 % at 20° / 15°).
- **The invoice number broke first:** 10 of 20 correct at 10/10 (6 wrong, 4 missed).
- At 20/15 the **invoice date** joined it (7 wrong).
- Total and vendor held up (20 / 19 of 20 at both angles).

The misreads also set off rules that have nothing to do with OCR: R-13 (a "date anomaly" from a misread date), R-08 (a
price variance), R-19 (a required field read as absent), and once **R-17**, a bank-detail change caused purely by
misread account digits. On every one of the same photos, the oracle rectification gave back exactly the baseline:
100 % accuracy, 0 escalations, 0 bad outcomes. It still kept the uneven light and the two resamplings.
**Recovery is 100 % at both angles, so by the pre-registered rule: build corner detection.** The OCR is fine on a flat
page, even a badly lit one; what it can't read is a keystoned one. The app's deskew only corrects in-plane rotation up
to 3°.

**The error-in-cleared column stayed at 0 %, but only because of R-20 (D-46).** In 5 photos (3 at 10/10, 2 at 20/15),
R-20 was the only finding on a document with a wrong field. Without it, those 5 would have auto-cleared wrong.

**What the 100 % does not promise.** A2 uses the true corners. A real detector will sometimes miss or misplace them,
so the build should be judged by how close it gets to A2, measured with this same harness: same 20 documents, detected
corners instead of `groundTruth`. These photos are also the easy case for corner detection: a white page with clean
edges on a dark wood desk, no motion blur, no sensor noise, no page curl. Those are separate axes, not measured here.
